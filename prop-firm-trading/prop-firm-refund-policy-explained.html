Prop Firm Refund Policies: When You Actually Get Your Evaluation Fee Back

Updated September 19, 2026

A prop-firm purchase is not automatically refundable after a pass, payout, cancellation, or rule breach. Current policies differ by provider, product, purchase date, promotion, account activity, jurisdiction, and payment type. The dated terms shown in your checkout and dashboard control.

Separate Four Different Money Events

Marketing pages often use similar language for different events. Identify which event the written policy actually covers:

EventQuestion to verify in current terms
Purchase refundCan the original evaluation or subscription charge be returned, and only before trading?
Rebill refundIs an accidental renewal eligible, within what request window, and only if unused?
Reset or activation feeIs the add-on refundable after it is applied or activated?
Payout or milestone benefitIs any fee credit tied to a payout, live transition, or another stated milestone?

Why a Universal Refund Timeline Is Misleading

There is no reliable industry-wide “typical” processing time. One current first-party policy reviewed for this page says eligible refunds return to the original payment method and may take 7–10 business days, while also making many purchases nonrefundable once service or trading access is used. Another provider may use different eligibility and timing.

Do not treat a card-network pending period, a support approval, and money posting to the account as the same timestamp. Save the approval reference and follow the provider's escalation path if the published period expires.

Refund Eligibility Checklist

  • Exact legal provider and product name
  • One-time purchase, subscription, rebill, reset, activation, data, or other add-on
  • Purchase date, policy version, and any promotion terms
  • Whether the account was traded, passed, failed, reset, activated, or paid out
  • Cancellation status and effective time
  • Required request channel, deadline, identity checks, and original payment method
  • Any chargeback, dispute, jurisdiction, or arbitration term

Passing Is Not the Same as a Refund

A pass can cancel a subscription, open an activation step, or change account status without returning the original evaluation fee. A payout can also be separate from billing. Verify each event in the current provider documentation instead of inferring a refund from words such as “funded,” “credited,” or “eligible.”

For related recurring-cost mechanics, see daily loss and reset rules. Provider rules can change without notice, so preserve the terms that applied to the actual purchase.

Confirm Before Paying

Treat the amount as nonrefundable unless the current written policy for the exact product clearly says otherwise. This page explains an audit process; it does not promise eligibility or a processing date.
