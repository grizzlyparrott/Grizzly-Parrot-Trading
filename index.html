Frequently asked questions

Using Grizzly Parrot Trading

What does Grizzly Parrot Trading teach?

Grizzly Parrot Trading teaches futures contract mechanics, tick and point value, position risk, orders, liquidity, market structure, expiration, prop-account drawdown, and practical platform workflows. The material is educational and does not provide individualized trade recommendations.

Where should a beginner start?

Start with what a futures contract represents, then learn contract specifications, ticks and dollar value, stop-based risk, order behavior, liquidity, and contract expiration. The Futures Basics hub presents those subjects in a structured order.

Are the calculators free to use?

Yes. The browser-based calculators and simulators on the site are free educational tools. They run client-side and are simplified planning aids, not brokerage systems, live market data, or guarantees of actual fills and results.

Does Grizzly Parrot Trading provide financial advice or trade calls?

No. The site provides general futures education and research frameworks, not individualized financial, investment, legal, or tax advice and not a trade-call service. Readers must verify current exchange and broker rules and decide whether futures are appropriate for them.

How are factual contract claims sourced?

Detailed guides cite first-party exchange, regulator, platform, or rulebook sources where factual specifications matter. Because contract terms, margin, schedules, and account rules can change, readers are directed to verify the current official source before acting.

Educational and risk disclaimer

Grizzly Parrot Trading provides general education, not individualized financial, investment, legal, or tax advice. Futures are leveraged, complex, and can produce losses beyond the amount initially deposited. Examples, diagrams, and calculators are simplified planning aids, not live market data, predictions, recommendations, or fill guarantees. Verify current exchange specifications, broker rules, and your own financial capacity before trading; use only risk capital you can afford to lose.
