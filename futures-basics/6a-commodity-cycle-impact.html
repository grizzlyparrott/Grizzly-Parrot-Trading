6A Commodity Cycles: Terms of Trade and AUD

By Kyle Parrott · Updated August 13, 2026

Commodity cycles can matter for 6A because Australia exports a large resource basket, not because an iron-ore candle remotely controls the futures contract. The defensible chain runs through export prices, the terms of trade, income, investment, fiscal conditions and interest-rate expectations. Every link has a lag, and every link can fail.

Direct answer

Commodity Prices Influence 6A Through the Australian Economy

A broad, sustained rise in the prices Australia receives for exports can improve the terms of trade and national-income outlook. That can alter business investment, government revenue, inflation risks and expectations for the Reserve Bank of Australia. If those changes improve Australia's outlook relative to the United States, demand for Australian dollars can strengthen. A decline can transmit in the opposite direction.

What is supported

A medium-run economic channel

The RBA identifies the terms of trade and interest-rate differentials as important influences on the Australian dollar. Its research also documents periods when the usual commodity-currency relationship weakened. That is exactly the point: the channel is real, but the price response is not a fixed coefficient.

What is not supported

A one-chart trading rule

"Commodity up, 6A up" ignores expectations, import costs, export volumes, hedging, the U.S. side of AUD/USD and common global shocks. It also confuses a macro explanation with an executable entry. Without a dated test on aligned data, it stays a hypothesis.

Quote orientation

Standard CME 6A is quoted in U.S. dollars per Australian dollar. A higher price means one Australian dollar buys more U.S. dollars. The contract is 100,000 AUD; its current outright Globex minimum increment is 0.00005, worth $5. A 0.0001 pip is two ticks and $10, not one current tick.

Transmission map

Follow the Links Instead of Naming a Commodity

A commodity move becomes relevant only after you specify who receives it, how long it lasts and what the market thinks it changes.

1

Price and volume

Separate the export price from shipped volume. Revenue can disappoint if a higher benchmark price arrives with weaker output, lower quality or disrupted demand.

2

Terms of trade

Compare export prices with import prices. An oil-price rise can help some Australian exports while also raising import costs; the net ratio matters.

3

Domestic pass-through

Ask whether the change affects profits, wages, investment, tax receipts, inflation or household demand. These channels operate at different speeds.

4

Relative repricing

Translate the Australian change into expected RBA policy and relative returns, then compare it with the U.S. outlook and broad dollar conditions.

The clean question

Do changes in a defined Australian export-price measure explain subsequent or simultaneous 6A returns after controlling for broad U.S.-dollar and risk factors? Name the measure, frequency, timestamps, contract-roll method, sample and controls before seeing the answer.

Measurement

Use Australia's Export Mix, Then Check What Changed

The RBA Index of Commodity Prices uses trade weights and groups rural, non-rural and base-metal exports. Australia's Department of Industry publishes current resource and energy forecasts, while the ABS reports actual international trade. Together they are more defensible than choosing whichever commodity recently matched the AUD chart.

Reproducible workflow

Turn the Story Into a Test You Can Audit

Do not optimize an entry while the economic variable is still vague. Build the evidence in this order.

Freeze the basket

Choose the RBA index or a predeclared subset. Record weights, currency denomination, publication lag and revision policy.

Use matched changes

For market co-movement, compare synchronized returns or price changes. Trending price levels can manufacture impressive-looking correlations.

Handle contracts

Use actual 6A contracts or disclose the continuous-series stitch. Roll returns and settlement gaps can contaminate a short sample.

Control common factors

Test broad dollar returns, equity or volatility conditions and relative rates. The same global shock can move commodities and 6A together.

Split regimes

Compare commodity booms, busts, policy cycles and calm or stressed markets using rules chosen before inspecting the results.

Report uncertainty

Publish sample size, window, coefficient variation, costs and failed specifications. One attractive chart is not validation.

Failure cases

When the Commodity Story Breaks, Do Not Force It

The RBA has documented a long stretch in which its usual commodity-price relationship with the Australian dollar weakened sharply. That history should kill the idea of a timeless multiplier.

Already expected

Futures reprice surprises. A strong published price or export number can coincide with a weaker AUD when positioning expected more.

Import offset

Higher export prices do less for the terms of trade if import prices rise too.

Volume deterioration

Weather, maintenance, policy or demand can reduce shipments while benchmark prices rise.

U.S. dominance

Fed repricing or a broad dollar move can overwhelm a modest Australian commodity impulse.

Risk liquidation

Funding stress can force cross-asset selling even when Australia's export prices remain firm.

Policy disconnect

The RBA may judge a commodity shock temporary or offset by weak domestic demand, limiting rate repricing.

Do not invent the lag

Daily, weekly and monthly economic series arrive on different clocks. Any claim that a particular commodity leads 6A by a fixed number of hours or days needs timestamped data, a predeclared lag family, out-of-sample testing and controls for shared news. This article makes no such performance claim.

Frequently asked questions

6A Commodity Cycles: Quick Answers

Do higher commodity prices always lift 6A?

No. Export prices can improve Australia's terms of trade and income outlook, but 6A also reflects what was expected, import prices, global risk, interest-rate expectations, the U.S. dollar and whether Australian producers capture the price move. Treat the commodity channel as conditional, not mechanical.

Which commodities matter most for the Australian dollar?

There is no permanent single answer. Iron ore, coal, LNG, gold and other exports matter in different weights and regimes. Start with the RBA's trade-weighted commodity index and current Australian export data instead of attaching 6A to one favorite chart.

What is Australia's terms of trade?

The terms of trade is the ratio of export prices to import prices. It can rise when Australia's export prices increase relative to what the country pays for imports. That can support national income, but it is not the same thing as export volume, company profit or an immediate 6A signal.

Should I trade 6A from a commodity chart alone?

No. First identify the economic channel, align timestamps and returns, test the relationship through multiple regimes, and check rates, broad U.S.-dollar conditions and risk markets. A commodity chart is context until a defined and validated rule makes it more than context.

Why can 6A ignore a commodity rally?

The move may already be priced, may be concentrated in an export with limited income pass-through, or may be offset by a stronger U.S. dollar, weaker global growth expectations, falling Australian relative yields or risk reduction. Divergence is information to investigate, not proof that one market is wrong.
