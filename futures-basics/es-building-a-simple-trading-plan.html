Decision rights · measurable rules · controlled change

Build an Auditable ES Trading Plan

By Kyle Parrott · Updated August 26, 2026

“Trade pullbacks, use discipline, stop after a bad day” is not a plan. Nobody can reconstruct what was permitted, what triggered the order or whether the stop was changed. An ES plan becomes useful when a second person can apply its rules to the same timestamped evidence and reach the same trade, wait or stop result.

One plan, one stated job

Define What the Plan Is Trying to Control

An objective should name the instrument, holding horizon, decision frequency and evidence standard. “Make money trading ES” cannot distinguish a process failure from ordinary outcome variance.

Unusable objective

Capture good moves

“Good,” “move” and “capture” have no timestamped definition. Any outcome can be rationalized afterward.

Auditable objective

Execute one defined setup

For example: test a named intraday setup in a declared session, with no hold through scheduled exclusions, fixed permission limits and order-level review.

A plan is not evidence of an edge.

It controls how a hypothesis is executed and measured. Profitability requires separate, forward-safe research with costs and out-of-sample evaluation. A clean plan can still produce a negative result.

A permission matrix should return a machine-like outcome: GO, REDUCE, WAIT or STOP. “Use judgment” may be unavoidable in a narrow field, but the plan should identify the evidence and who has authority to exercise that judgment.

Turn the setup into observable fields

Write a Setup Card That Can Be Replayed

The setup card separates facts available before the order from labels assigned afterward. Every level and state needs a calculation clock, source and expiry.

Hypothetical setup card

Context: exact dated ES contract; session window; no excluded event. Candidate: price returns to a timestamped reference after a forward-defined impulse. Trigger: a specified close or order-flow condition known at bar completion. Invalidation: a predeclared price proving the setup no longer applies. Expiry: cancel if not triggered by the stated clock or if market quality fails.

What must already be true?

Dated contract, session, event state, market quality, volatility state and any directional filter.

Trigger

What new observation acts?

Exact price, timestamp, completion rule and permitted order type. No future bar may redefine the trigger.

Invalidation

What proves the idea wrong?

A market observation independent of the desired contract count. It is not “when the loss feels large.”

Expiry

When does the idea become stale?

Time, event, roll, liquidity or structural change that cancels the setup even without a fill.

A signal does not choose an order

Write the Execution Branches Before Entry

CME Globex supports defined order types, while platforms may add synthetic behavior. The plan must describe the actual exchange or broker order, its trigger, price protection, duration and failure branch.

Entry condition passes

Is price control more important than immediacy?

Yes: use the specified limit logic, accept non-fill and set cancellation time. No: use only an approved executable order with a maximum cost and protection rule.

Partial fill

Is partial quantity permitted?

Yes: recalculate risk from actual average fill and protect filled quantity. No: cancel remainder and follow the written flatten-or-keep branch.

Stop trigger

Did the exit behave as modeled?

Yes: record trigger-to-fill slippage. No: follow emergency exposure reduction; do not widen the stop to preserve the thesis.

  • Before submit: symbol, side, quantity, order type, price, duration and attached orders read back correctly.
  • After submit: exchange or broker acknowledgement received; rejects and pending states handled.
  • After fill: actual average price, remaining quantity and protective order reconcile.
  • After cancel: acknowledgement proves no working order remains.
  • After exit: account position is flat or equals the explicitly approved remainder.

“Stay calm” is not an incident procedure.

Name the action, account, communication route, authority and evidence. If the plan assumes a tool remains available during the failure that disables it, the branch is not operational.

Change one controlled version at a time

Freeze the Plan Before Evaluating It

  1. Assign an ID.Version, effective date, instrument set, data sources and author.
  2. State the hypothesis.What behavior is expected and what would falsify it after costs?
  3. Freeze the rules.Do not edit triggers or exclusions after seeing the same-period outcomes.
  4. Collect a declared sample.Include rejects, misses, partials, incidents and no-trades.
  5. Evaluate separately.Measure adherence, cost estimate error and outcome uncertainty.
  6. Retire or revise.Create a new version with a reason; preserve the old record unchanged.

Minimum viable plan state

Simple means few rules, not vague rules

A production-quality first plan may trade only one setup, one session and one product. It still needs complete permissions, sizing, execution, incident and review branches before live risk.
