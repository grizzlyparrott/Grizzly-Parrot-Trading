Six Ideas That Make a Futures Quote Usable

A screen symbol is not enough. Before interpreting price, convert the contract into units, time, obligations, and dollars.

01 · Contract

A standardized agreement

The exchange defines the underlying reference, contract unit, price quotation, listed months, settlement method, and other obligations.

02 · Multiplier

The amount behind one price unit

The multiplier or contract unit converts a quoted move into notional exposure. Similar-looking prices can carry radically different dollar risk.

03 · Tick

The minimum allowed price increment

Tick size is the price step. Tick value is that step translated into dollars per contract. Always verify both from the current exchange specification.

04 · Margin

A performance bond, not the purchase price

Initial and maintenance margin support the position. Broker intraday requirements can differ and change; neither number defines the trade's maximum loss.

05 · Settlement

How obligations resolve

Futures are marked to market and eventually expire. Final settlement can involve cash or physical delivery under product-specific procedures.

06 · Liquidity

The ability to execute at a tolerable cost

Volume helps, but spread, depth, queue, order size, volatility, and time of day determine the fill available when the order actually arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Futures Basics Questions

What should a beginner learn first about futures?

Start with what a futures contract represents, then verify its contract unit, quotation, tick size, tick value, expiration, settlement type, and trading hours. After that, learn dollar risk, margin, order behavior, liquidity, and roll mechanics before studying setups.

How is futures trade risk calculated?

A practical estimate is stop distance in ticks multiplied by tick value and contracts, plus expected commissions and slippage. Choose the technically valid stop before contract count, and reduce the position or skip if even one contract exceeds the risk budget.

Is futures margin the maximum amount a trader can lose?

No. Futures margin is a performance-bond requirement, not a maximum-loss figure or the purchase price of the underlying asset. Leveraged losses can exceed the initial amount deposited.

Do micro futures automatically make a trade low risk?

No. A micro contract usually reduces dollar exposure per tick relative to a larger related contract, but total risk still depends on stop distance, number of contracts, volatility, slippage, fees, and available account or drawdown buffer.

What happens when a futures contract expires?

Before expiration, a trader generally closes, rolls, or holds into settlement subject to the contract and broker rules. Settlement can be physical or cash-based. Exact last-trade dates, delivery procedures, and broker cutoffs vary by product and contract month.

Educational and risk disclaimer

This page provides general education, not individualized financial, investment, legal, or tax advice. Futures are leveraged, complex, and can produce losses beyond the amount initially deposited. Examples and calculators are simplified planning aids, not predictions or fill guarantees. Verify current exchange specifications, broker rules, and your own financial capacity before trading; use only risk capital you can afford to lose.
