6S Macro Triggers: A State-Dependent Triage Framework

By Kyle Parrott · Updated August 19, 2026

There is no timeless leaderboard of events that move the Swiss franc “most.” An expected SNB decision can matter less than an unanticipated funding shock; a large payroll headline can matter less than its revisions; a geopolitical event can be absorbed quickly or transform the entire policy outlook. Priority comes from surprise, channel relevance, live state, liquidity and confirmation—not the event name alone.

Scheduled policy interpretation belongs to the SNB decision framework; shock classification belongs to the global-risk scenario map. This page owns only the cross-trigger triage.

Information is relative

Score Relevance Only After Freezing the Prior

A large number can be fully expected, while a small wording change can alter a policy path. Use qualitative grades only when their definitions are fixed before the outcome; do not convert them into unsupported return forecasts.

Trigger intake record

Store the primary release and exact timestamp; contemporaneous expectation and source; previous official vintage; changed fields and revisions; expected channel; scheduled overlaps; quote-correct 6S state; active contract; and market-quality limits. Unscheduled shocks use the last verified pre-shock state as the prior and explicitly mark what was unknowable.

A trigger matters through a route

Map the Candidate Channel Before Looking for Confirmation

The same release can affect several routes in opposing directions. State the required chain and an independent invalidator for each.

Trigger versus priorEvent name alone has no sign.

SNB–Fed path

Requires comparable, timestamp-aligned curve repricing at the thesis horizon. Reject if the relative move is absent or caused by a rival event.

Broad-dollar

Requires USD breadth across non-circular measures. Reject if CHF-specific crosses explain the move better.

Haven and funding

Requires evidence about portfolios, collateral or currency funding. Reject a generic risk label when the funding currency points elsewhere.

Intervention constraint

Requires official stance or delayed transaction evidence. Reject price-only claims about an SNB order.

Fact: CME quotes 6S in USD per CHF, so higher 6S is consistent with CHF appreciation versus USD. Inference: the cause of that move remains unidentified until the registered channel evidence appears.

Priority is conditional and temporary

State dependence

Current inflation, growth, funding and policy constraints determine which trigger has relevant information.

Liquidity dependence

A high-information event can still be ineligible when spread, depth, gap or latency violates the plan.

Horizon dependence

A release can dominate minutes while having little verified relevance to the registered multi-day decision.

Expiry

Priority ends at a declared checkpoint. It is not carried forward until a later move makes it look right.

Close the loop

Review the Trigger Without Ranking From the Outcome

A large move does not prove the event deserved high ex-ante priority; a small move does not prove it was unimportant. Compare the frozen triage record with what was observable at each checkpoint.

1

Preserve the intake

Hash or archive the prior, release, trigger class, channel and initial state.

2

Audit the clock

Separate first response, later reversal, policy follow-up and contract-roll effects.

3

Grade the mechanism

Confirmed, mixed, rejected or unidentified—never “right” from price alone.

4

Grade execution separately

Eligible, blocked or failed after actual spread, depth, latency, slippage and risk controls.

Research boundary

This framework does not publish a historical ranking of SNB, FOMC, CPI, payroll, geopolitical or liquidity events. Such a ranking would require a fully specified, versioned event study with uncertainty, controls and out-of-sample stability.

Triage never replaces contract due diligence. The canonical 6S contract-specification guide owns quotation, tick arithmetic, listed months, termination, physical delivery, roll risk and the distinction between exchange performance bond and broker day margin. Before declaring any trigger action-eligible, translate the intended stop and size into verified dollars, identify the exact delivery month, and reject the order when basis, spread, depth or roll migration makes the observed 6S response non-comparable. A high-information event can still require no trade.
Educational and risk disclaimer

This triage framework is general education; it does not recommend a position or replace legal, tax, accounting, hedging, financial or brokerage advice. Event gaps, leverage, spread, slippage, basis, rolls and delivery exposure can produce losses beyond initial margin.
