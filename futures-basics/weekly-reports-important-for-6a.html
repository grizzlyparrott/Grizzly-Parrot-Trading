6A Economic Calendar: A Weekly Preparation Guide

By Kyle Parrott · Updated August 13, 2026

A weekly 6A plan is not a list of reports that happen every week. It is the work of collecting the actual RBA, ABS, Federal Reserve, U.S., China, commodity, positioning and contract events landing during the next seven days.

Direct answer

Most Important 6A Catalysts Are Not Weekly Releases

Australian employment, official China PMI, U.S. payrolls and many inflation reports are monthly. RBA and FOMC decisions follow scheduled meeting calendars. GDP and some price or wage measures are quarterly. Weekly U.S. claims and CFTC reports can matter, but neither controls 6A every week. Build the actual calendar; do not invent cadence.

Calendar job

Locate risk windows

The calendar tells you when new official information or contract mechanics may change volatility and liquidity. It does not predict direction. The 6A driver map explains the potential transmission channels.

Trading job

Define actions before the window

Decide whether to avoid entry, reduce size, monitor a reaction or do nothing. "Important" does not mean "must trade." A no-trade outcome is often the cleanest execution of the plan.

Forecast commentary is not an official release

Analysts can update views any day. Label those as research or market expectations. Do not rename them "weekly Australian employment" or "weekly China PMI" and present them as scheduled government data.

Frequency does not rank market impact

A weekly release can produce little change, while an unscheduled policy headline can dominate the entire week. Cadence tells you when information arrives, not how far 6A will move.

Event ranking

Give Each Event a Mechanism, a Horizon and a Rejection Test

Do not assign "high impact" because a calendar icon is red. Write why the event could change AUD versus USD and what evidence would show that interpretation failed.

1

Name the mechanism

Relative RBA-Fed policy, Australian demand, China-linked exports, global risk, positioning or contract mechanics.

2

Define the expected surprise

Record available consensus and prior values without treating either as certain.

3

Choose the evidence

Comparable rates, relevant commodities, broad risk and active-contract behavior should have separate jobs.

4

Write the rejection test

If rates and 6A refuse the proposed chain or the report is mixed, lower confidence instead of inventing hidden motives.

Weekly workflow

Build Once, Recheck Daily, Update When the Source Changes

The workflow is deliberately boring. Boring is useful when several time zones, contract dates and economic releases can collide.

  1. Inventory official events.Pull the next seven days from RBA, ABS, Fed, BLS, China NBS, CFTC and CME sources.
  2. Normalize time zones.Keep the publisher's local time and add your execution time with the date. Account for daylight-saving changes.
  3. Add contract events.Record active 6A month, holidays, roll, delivery and broker deadlines.
  4. Rank mechanisms.Assign one primary channel and avoid double-counting correlated events.
  5. Define risk windows.Set entry restrictions, size changes, spread limits and no-trade conditions before the event.
  6. Recheck each day.Confirm dates, postponements, corrections and unscheduled policy communication from the primary source.
  7. Capture outcomes.Archive official releases, relative rates and the executable 6A response for review.
Turn a scheduled catalyst into a position only through the falsifiable 6A macro-thesis workflow, then size it with the 6A risk process. A calendar entry alone is not a setup.

Failure cases

A Beautiful Calendar Can Still Produce a Bad Trade

Preparation reduces avoidable surprises. It does not reveal the future reaction.

Wrong frequency

Calling monthly employment or PMI "weekly" creates events that do not exist.

Wrong time zone

Canberra, Beijing, New York and Chicago dates can straddle your local day.

Stale calendar

Holidays, delays and corrections can invalidate a copied schedule.

Importance inflation

Listing every release as high impact produces no prioritization and encourages overtrading.

First-candle certainty

Mixed details, revisions, rates and liquidity can reverse the initial move.

Missing contract context

A roll or thin month can distort the chart during an otherwise ordinary week.

Unscheduled risk stays unscheduled

Geopolitical events, policy remarks, interventions, commodity disruptions and funding shocks do not wait for your calendar. Size and contingency planning still matter when the listed week looks empty.

Frequently asked questions

6A Economic Calendar: Quick Answers

Which weekly reports always move 6A futures?

None always move 6A. Initial U.S. jobless claims and CFTC reports have weekly publication schedules, but major Australian, U.S. and China catalysts are often monthly, quarterly or policy-event based. Impact depends on surprise, revisions, regime, timing and prior pricing.

Is Australian employment data released every week?

No. Labour Force, Australia is a monthly ABS release. Forecast updates or media commentary are not substitutes for the official report, and the current ABS calendar should be checked because publication dates can change.

Is China's official PMI a weekly report?

No. China's National Bureau of Statistics publishes its official Purchasing Managers' Index monthly under a published release calendar. Private surveys and analyst forecast changes must be labeled separately from official NBS data.

How far ahead should a 6A trader build the calendar?

Build the next week before it begins, recheck official sources each day, and look farther ahead for RBA, FOMC, expiry and holiday risk. Calendars can change, so a saved screenshot is not final authority.

What makes a scheduled event relevant to 6A?

The event needs a plausible channel into the relative Australian-versus-U.S. outlook, commodity demand, global risk or contract liquidity. Rank the expected surprise and timing, then require market evidence instead of assuming every listed release deserves a trade.
